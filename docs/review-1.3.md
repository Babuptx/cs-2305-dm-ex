# Section 1.3 review record

On October 12, 2026, the owner explicitly requested building and publishing Section 1.3. The lesson is published for owner review, with no editorial status labels in the reader interface. Internal lesson and unit metadata remains in progress until review. Section 1.4 has not been requested.

Coverage: twelve companion teaching units cover Rosen eighth edition §1.3.1–§1.3.7, exposition on printed pages 26–37. Topics include tautologies, contradictions and contingencies; equivalence versus a biconditional; the standard equivalence laws; De Morgan's laws and grouped expressions; conditional and biconditional replacements; justified equivalence chains; satisfiability; exactly-one constraints; queens and Sudoku encodings; and choosing a proof or search method. The lesson includes three truth tables, 32 reference identities with original explanations, four worked proof tables, original small puzzle examples, and expandable checks. Exercises on printed pages 38–40 are not reproduced as an answer bank.

Editorial review by the authoring assistant used the existing cached source extraction and visual inspection of relevant original pages. The lesson separates agreement on one assignment from logical equivalence, and separates one satisfying assignment from a tautology or a unique solution. The queens model consistently uses row and column coordinates, correct same-diagonal conditions, and both parts of exactly-one constraints. The Sudoku model deliberately makes every cell/row/column/block exactly-one condition explicit, using a 4 × 4 example and explaining how variables scale to 9 × 9. This is an authoring review, not an independent human subject-matter review.

Source discrepancies checked against the actual PDF: the printed page 34 diagonal statement and row-pair formula contain inconsistent indices. The companion uses |i − k| = |j − l| for distinct squares and pairs Q(i, j) with Q(i, k) within a row. The opening of §1.3.7 on printed page 37 misstates the relationship between satisfiability and the negation being a tautology; the correct relationship, also stated in §1.3.5, is that A is unsatisfiable exactly when ¬A is a tautology. The companion gives both that statement and its satisfiable/not-tautology counterpart.

The queens check compares pair clauses with an independent geometric attack test. These tests establish the checked computations, not the completeness or teaching quality of the prose.
