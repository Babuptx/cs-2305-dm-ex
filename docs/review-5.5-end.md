# Section 5.5 and Chapter 5 review record

The owner asked to finish the last two Chapter 5 entries after publication of Sections 5.3 and 5.4. This release completes the available chapter map with Section 5.5, Program Correctness, and End-of-Chapter Material. They have twelve and fourteen teaching units respectively. Internal editorial metadata stays in progress pending owner review; reader pages contain no editorial status labels. This request does not authorize work on Chapter 6 or other unwritten chapters.

Section 5.5 covers Rosen eighth edition §5.5.1–§5.5.5, printed pages 393–398: specifications, preconditions and postconditions, partial versus total correctness, Hoare triples, assignment, composition, conditionals, loop invariants, factorial verification, and signed multiplication by repeated addition. Companion extensions explain backward reasoning for assignments, implication directions when changing assertions, and termination measures. The notation `{p} S {q}` is explicitly mapped to the textbook's `p{S}q`. An original diagram shows initialization, preservation, exit, and the separate termination obligation. The factorial example starts at zero and therefore includes 0!, while the source's positive-input version starts at one.

The end-of-chapter page follows the purposes of the source's Key Terms and Results, Review Questions, Supplementary Exercises, Computer Projects, Computations and Explorations, and Writing Projects on pages 398–404. It provides original review material, not a reproduction or complete solution bank for the source's numbered exercises. Coverage includes a chapter map and glossary, method selection, worked sum/inequality/divisibility practice, strong induction and least-counterexample arguments, a structural tree proof, recursive algorithm traces, a division loop capstone, proof repair, coding briefs, exact arithmetic and Fibonacci parity explorations, research prompts, and final retrieval checks. Project briefs are learner activities, not claims that every project implementation or research essay has been supplied.

Editorial checks used the original PDF text on pages 393–404 and visual inspection of pages 394, 396, 397, 399, and 404. Reviewed details include old versus new assignment values, both branch paths and equality boundaries, invariant checkpoint timing, input domains and preserved parameters, initialization and exit implications, zero-iteration cases, all sign combinations for multiplication, and integer-valued decreasing termination measures. The review distinguishes exact arithmetic from machine arithmetic, tests from proofs, and partial correctness from a useful terminating solution. In practice proofs, induction bases cover all needed residues, the strong hypothesis uses only allowed smaller indices, and full-tree counts depend on both children. Factorial digit thresholds are strict, and the parity pattern is justified by recurrence of a deterministic pair state.

Eleven new tests in `tests/correctness.test.mjs` bring the repository total to 63 passing tests. Tests execute the actual published assignment, branch, factorial, signed-multiplication, and division snippets in an isolated interpreter with a timeout and loop-step cap. They check loop invariants at every guard evaluation, including the final exit, and verify decreasing measures and independent expected outputs. Faulty examples hit the step cap as expected. Other finite tests cover review sum arithmetic, divisibility, bounds, bundle constructions, full binary tree counts, exact factorial digits, and Fibonacci parity. These finite tests supplement the written proof review; they are not formal proofs of all-input correctness.
